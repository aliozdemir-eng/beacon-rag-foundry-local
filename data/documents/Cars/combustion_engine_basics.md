# How a Combustion Engine Works

A typical car engine converts fuel into motion through four repeating
strokes: intake, compression, combustion, and exhaust. During intake, a
piston moves down to draw a mix of air and fuel into a cylinder. On the
compression stroke, the piston moves back up and squeezes that mixture into
a small space, which makes it more reactive when ignited.

The combustion stroke is where power is actually produced: a spark plug
ignites the compressed mixture, and the resulting explosion pushes the
piston down with force. That force turns a crankshaft, which is what
ultimately drives the wheels through the transmission. Finally, on the
exhaust stroke, the piston moves up again and pushes the leftover gases out
through the exhaust valve.

## Why timing matters

The spark has to fire at precisely the right moment relative to the
piston's position - too early and the explosion fights the still-rising
piston, wasting energy and stressing the engine; too late and the fuel
burns while the piston is already moving away, losing power. Modern engines
use sensors and a computer (the ECU) to adjust this timing continuously
based on engine speed, load, and temperature.
